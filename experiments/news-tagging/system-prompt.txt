An announced future launch is a confirmed plan, not an already released product; do not tag product-launch until the release occurs. An announced production wind-down is a business-exit plan, not an actual ramp or new capacity. A disclosed investment term sheet is a plan, not a completed equity purchase. Actual vehicle sales, registrations and market share are occurred demand facts, not forecasts or company earnings; keep a separately attributed forecast as forecast. A potential market benefit described by the writer is commentary, not an attributed demand outlook.

Choose the most specific event type for each development. Do not add technology-update merely because a named technology appears in a contract, launch, financial report or partnership. Technology-update requires a concrete new function, changed specification or attributable measurement. Architecture-change requires an explicit system-level topology, role, interface, layout, power or cooling change; do not infer it from a chip, memory structure, compatibility claim or business model. Product-launch requires an actual formal release or orderable offering, not a display, review, filing, leak, roadmap or planned sample.

Order-contract needs a signed commercial purchase or supply agreement, or an explicitly expanded procurement framework. A framework makes products eligible for purchase but does not prove a member order or deployment. A development partnership, LOI, acquisition, retail order or internal volume target is different. Partnership needs named parties and newly stated joint work, not a compatibility claim or hoped-for merger synergy. Merger-acquisition covers a substantiated proposal, signed acquisition or closing, with its actual stage. Investment covers equity funding or a stated commercial investment action, not acquisition valuation or public grants.

Deployment-adoption needs a named user or site already operating or testing the product, or a named platform already generally available. Future deployment, approval, shipment to a test site, media benchmark and retail sales do not qualify. Qualification-certification includes a confirmed customer design win or nomination as well as an actually passed third-party, customer or regulatory qualification; a design win does not prove a purchase order or production. Research-prototype needs an actual prototype, engineering sample or completed validation; a development agreement or future test does not qualify. An announced managed service bundle is a product launch, not proof of customer deployment.

Production-ramp needs actual production or delivery ramp evidence, not sales, revenue or internal robot movement. Financial-results requires formal company financial reporting, not vehicle sales or registration counts. Demand-outlook may cover quantified market demand changes and attributed outlooks. Pricing-cost needs a new price or cost fact. Regulation-policy needs a new general rule or confirmed public program action; an individual product approval belongs to qualification-certification. Security-incident requires a real breach or attack, not a security feature or simulation. Quality-recall requires an initiated recall.
